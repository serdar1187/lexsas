Composing your own agentic workflow: what the canvas gives a legal team

A composed workflow gives a legal team three things a bought one cannot: its own stages, a mandate it can read, and records it owns. The fourth thing it demands is discipline.

Legal AI: build, buy, or configure?, published here earlier this month, decides which path fits a use case. This piece is about what comes after that decision, because the benefit lives there. When I compose a workflow for a legal team, the first thing we draw is not the automation; it is the team's own day: where matters arrive, who reads first, where the file waits and for whom. A bought product ships someone else's answer to those questions. A composed agentic workflow ships yours, and that is the first return: nothing about the team has to bend.

The second return is control you can see. On the canvas, every tool the agent holds, every source it may read, every place it may write is a drawn connection that a lawyer can point at, question and remove. Meta's Agents Rule of Two caps an agent at two of three risky properties in a session; drawn as a workflow, that cap stops being a vendor's assurance and becomes a picture the general counsel can read. Security review turns from a questionnaire into a mandate you set yourself.

For an in-house team the return shows up in three places. Hours: routine intake and first-pass review run themselves to the gate where a person signs. Custody: the run history stays in your perimeter, a record you can show a regulator or the board without asking anyone's permission. Speed of change: a stage gets redrawn in a day, not in a vendor's release cycle. A law firm adds a fourth: each practice group gets a workflow shaped to its own review depth, and the same records let partners price the work rather than defend the hours.

None of it is free. A composed workflow is owned, and owned means maintained: every model, prompt or connector change passes a release gate, and the workflow definition joins the assets you would take with you on exit. That is also the honest test of the road itself: the teams that gain most from owning the canvas are the ones prepared to own the discipline. Which side of that line a team stands on is worth a conversation before it is worth a pilot.
